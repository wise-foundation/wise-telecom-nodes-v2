# Architecture for Developers

The components, the data flow and the rules that keep every chain in agreement.

The referral system is three sets of contracts around the running vault diamonds: new facets on every vault, one registry per chain and one hub on Ethereum mainnet. This chapter describes each component, the path a deposit takes until it becomes a claim, the rules of the registry, the bounds on every loop, the levers we hold and the storage discipline. Paths are relative to the repository root. Contracts that have not been broadcast yet live in `pending/` folders; a file leaves such a folder only after a verified broadcast.

The signature of every view and event is in [UI Integration](ui-integration.md); the user facing functions are in [For Integrators and Partners](for-integrators.md).

## The Components

### The Vault Diamonds and the Cohort Facets

Every Wise Telecom Nodes vault is a selector routed diamond. The referral system adds facets to it; **nothing already deployed is edited.** The new facets live in `src/diamond/vault/facets/pending/`.

* **`ReferralFacet` and `Permit2ReferralFacet`:** the referral variants of the deposit entrypoints. Their shared step, in `ReferralFacetBase`, forwards the pair `(msg.sender, referrer)` to the chain's registry through the vault's registry pointer and lets the registry apply the write once rule. `getReferrerOf` reads the registry through the same pointer.
* **The cohort recorder:** the engine that measures stake, compiled into three engine slices that execute through the vault's transfer hook pointer and the dispatcher. `CohortHookFacet` is the transfer hook slice, installed as the vault's `transferHookFacet`, carrying the sync, credit, advance and settle entrypoints; `CohortMoveHookFacet` is the same chain move carry; `CohortBridgeHookFacet` is the bridge carry and the referrer flip. They share the bases `CohortRecorderBase`, `CohortClampBase`, `CohortLaneBase`, `CohortBookBase` and `CohortMoveCarryBase`, and `CohortPinnedBase` pins the genesis storage slots they touch so the slices stay small. The engine never reverts inside the transfer hook: lanes saturate and report through events.
* **`CohortRecorderFacet`:** the routed public surface of the recorder: the permissionless clamp poke `syncReferee`, the permissionless bounded pre walk `advanceBook`, the registry only `receiveReferrerFlip`, the master only `seedCohortContribution` and the read views.
* **`CohortFlushFacet` with `CohortFlushPlanner` and `CohortFlushTransport`:** ships the books to the hub. `CohortFlushPolicyFacet` holds the flush bot allowlist, the public flush switch and the CCIP gas limit of the flush message, with their read views.
* **`AdminFacet`:** carries the pointer to the hub and its three day change timelock (`proposeFlushHub`, `executeFlushHubChange`, `cancelFlushHubChange`, `getFlushHub`), the registry pointer and the arming switch `setCohortSyncEnabled`.

### The Shared Libraries

`src/diamond/shared/pending/CohortWeeks.sol` holds the week epoch, the lane geometry and the carry codecs that the vault facets and the hub share, so no contract restates these numbers. The week is `block.timestamp / 1 weeks`; the ring has 52 slots; the year is 52 weeks; a book word and a crumb word hold three 80 bit lanes (year one, year two, mature); a crumb word also sets bit 252 when written and carries `CRUMB_VERSION` 1.

`src/diamond/shared/pending/CohortFlushCodec.sol` defines the flush envelope: a packed sequence of 32 byte words without ABI framing, `FLUSH_VERSION` 2, at most `MAX_FLUSH_ENTRIES` (16) entries and `MAX_FLUSH_WORK` (160) work units, a crumb, a self week and a gap week costing one unit each.

### The Registry

`src/registry/pending/ReferralRegistry.sol` is the per chain referrer registry, one standalone contract per chain, built from `ReferralRegistryDeclarations` (storage, events, errors), `ReferralRegistryHelper` (the checks, the merge and the CCIP transport) and `ReferralRegistryAdmin` (the master lanes). Every vault on the chain reads and writes the same registry. The registry is its own CCIP endpoint for the referrer sync between chains and for the payout designation push to the hub, it stages the same chain move carry between two vaults, and it holds the correction lane.

### The Hub

`src/hub/pending/ReferralHub.sol` is the mainnet hub, a diamond of its own with five facets that share the `ReferralHubDeclarations` layout.

* **`ReferralHubCheckpointsFacet`:** the week bucket reads (`ladderInputsAt`, `weekReportOf`, `weekSelfOf`).
* **`ReferralHubRatesFacet`:** the rate reads and the master levers `pushRateTable`, `setDelivererShareCap`, `elevateReferrer` and `endElevation`.
* **`ReferralHubIntakeFacet`:** the reporter surface for the mainnet vaults (`reportSelf`, `deliverCrumbs`, `deliverGap`), the permissionless `settleWeeks`, the source and cursor reads, and the levers `setAllowedReporter` and `setSourceSystem`.
* **`ReferralHubMailboxFacet`:** the CCIP receive, the permissionless `applyFlush`, and the levers `setCcipRouter`, `setFlushSource` and `setDesignationSource`.
* **`ReferralHubPayoutFacet`:** the claim rail (`claim`, `claimUpTo`, `claimAll`), the hub chain `setPayoutAddress` and `payoutAddressOf`.

The entry contract holds the constructor (which seeds the genesis rate table), the two step ownership, the selector timelock, the lever change proposals and the fallback dispatcher. The internal chain `ReferralHubDeclarations` to `ReferralHubCheckpoints` to `ReferralHubRates` to `ReferralHubIntake` to `ReferralHubMailbox` is compiled into the facets that need it. `IReferralHub` is the external surface of the deployed diamond. `ReferralHubReader` is a stateless read companion deployed as a second unit; it reads only the hub's primitive getters, so it can be redeployed at any time.

## How it Works:

### From a Deposit to a Claim:

1. **The deposit names a referrer.** A depositor calls a referral deposit entrypoint. The facet calls `assignReferrer` on the registry. The registry refuses a zero or self referrer, the registry itself, the hub, any address ever allowed as a vault and any referrer whose chain leads back to the user, writes `referrerOf[user]` once, stamps `referrerKey[user]` and emits `ReferralAssigned`. A user who already has a referrer is silently kept. The deposit then runs as a plain deposit.
2. **The deposit credits the recorder.** The transfer hook slice resolves the user's referrer through a guarded staticcall to the registry (`effectiveReferrerOf`, which answers the old referrer while a flip is pending), raises the user's `trackedContribution`, and credits the referrer's book: the current slot of the year one ring and the book's year one lane, plus the user's `attributedContribution`. A deposit without a referrer tracks but attributes nothing; the own stake still feeds the self input.
3. **Every later touchpoint settles first.** A settle walks the week boundaries crossed since the last settle, at most 104 per call, rotates the rings at each boundary (year one into year two, year two into mature), writes one crumb per completed week into `cohortWeekLog`, and compresses a dormant tail into a gap record in `cohortGapLog`. The referrer's own stake is settled into a per account self crumb, and every completed week appends a self log record.
4. **Movements clamp.** A transfer, a withdrawal, a queue join or a burn runs the one way clamp: the user's tracked contribution falls to the balance held, and the referrer's book is charged at most `min(deficit, attributed)` through the sale walk, newest first through both rings and then the mature lane. A sender the hook cannot clamp in place is parked in one slot and drained at the next entrypoint. A same chain move captures the taken ring slots as pairs, stages them in the registry (`stageCarry`) and the destination consumes them in the same transaction (`consumeCarry`); a bridge packs the pairs into the bridge message and the destination lands them under its own registry's answer. Every carried pair lands at the slot of its own credit week, aged only by the week boundaries crossed in flight.
5. **A flush ships closed weeks.** `flushReferrals` or `flushReferralWindow` settles the book, plans the entries from storage (closed weeks only: the crumb of each week, the week's own self presence as runs of equal weekly averages, gap slices for dormant spans, and the referrer's payout designation read from the registry in CCIP mode) and sends one envelope. On a spoke chain the envelope goes to the hub over CCIP; on mainnet the facet calls the hub's reporter surface directly, entry by entry.
6. **The hub prices.** The mailbox stores a commitment for every received envelope and anyone applies it with `applyFlush`. Each entry reports its self runs, its crumbs and its gap weeks into the week buckets, prices each (source, referrer, week) cell against its cumulative cursor at the week's current rates, credits the referrer's claimable bucket for the source's chain, and pays the courier share when a deliverer is set.
7. **The referrer claims.** `claim`, `claimUpTo` or `claimAll` transfer USDC from the hub's own balance to the bucket's payout address.

### The Registry: Keys and the Merge Rule

A sync key is `(assignedAt << 8) | originChainRank`. Each registry is constructed with its chain's rank (mainnet 0, Base 1, Arbitrum 2, Robinhood 3) and stamps every local assignment with the current timestamp and that rank. **The lower key wins a merge; a lower rank wins a tie.**

Keys travel two ways. `broadcastSync(user, chainSelectors)` is permissionless and caller paid: it sends the user's current pair and key to the listed peer registries over CCIP. A bridge of the user's shares carries the origin key in the bridge carry payload, and the destination vault merges it into its registry through `mergeReferrer`.

`_mergeReferrerPair` is the only place a referrer changes without the user's deposit. It refuses a key whose timestamp is more than one hour ahead of the local clock, whose timestamp mirrored against the local clock falls below `SYNC_KEY_MIRROR`, or whose rank is above `maxSyncRank` (three at construction, raised only by master before a fifth chain is peered). A user with no local key adopts the incoming pair. An incoming key equal to or above the local key changes nothing; an equal key naming a different referrer only emits `SyncKeyCollision`. A strictly lower key with the same referrer lowers the stored key. A strictly lower key with a different referrer flips the user: the registry rewrites `referrerOf`, marks a pending flip on every tracked vault that holds attributed contribution for the user, and emits `ReferralSyncFlipped`. A mutual pair resolves by key as well: the smaller key dissolves the losing edge, adopts the winner and drops any pending correction proposal of the dissolved user.

A flip is delivered lazily. `effectiveReferrerOf(user, vault)` answers the old referrer for a vault with a pending flip marker, so every interim clamp and credit hits the book that holds the cover. The permissionless `pokeMigration(user)` (or `pokeMigrationAt(user, vault)` for one vault) calls `receiveReferrerFlip` on each marked vault: the vault settles the old book, charges the user's attributed slice out of it and replants its pairs at their own slots in the new referrer's rings. A disarmed vault defers the flip and keeps the marker; a vault without a book for the user consumes it.

A correction mints a mirrored key `((SYNC_KEY_MIRROR - block.timestamp) << 8) | rank`, which sorts below every genuine key, so a later correction sorts below an earlier one and a corrected pair wins every merge against every copy of the old pair. One `broadcastSync` after the execute carries it to every peer. The merge floor admits every genuine key and every correction executed at most one hour ahead of the local clock. A proposal is bound to the exact key held when it was made (`proposedCorrectionKey`), so any flip, key lowering or first assignment during the window makes the execute revert with `NothingToCorrect`. A correction of an existing pair must be proposed on the chain whose rank the held key carries (`WrongCorrectionChain` elsewhere); an assignment of a user without a referrer may be proposed on any chain. `latchCorrections` is a manual one way close of the lane; `latchSeeds` is the one way close of the retroactive seed, separate from it.

**Allowed vaults.** Before `finalizeSyncSetup` we allow a vault instantly with `setAllowedVault`; afterwards a new address takes `proposeAllowedVault`, three days, `executeAllowedVaultChange`, and re allowing a vault the registry ever allowed stays instant. A revoke is instant and never removes the vault from the tracked list, which is monotone and holds at most `MAX_TRACKED_VAULTS` (8) addresses, because every vault that ever held book credit must keep receiving flip markers. The ever allowed flag also bars a vault from ever being a referrer or designating a payout. The CCIP router and every sync peer change through the same propose, three day, execute shape once the sync setup is finalized; `removeSyncPeer` is instant, cancels any pending proposal for that lane and raises the lane's quarantine flag, which the vaults read before merging a bridge carried pair from that chain.

### The Vault Flush: Cursors and Work Caps

Each referrer has two send cursors on each vault. `flushedThroughWeek` is the last closed week whose crumb was shipped, and `flushGapCursor` packs the index of the next gap record to ship in its low 128 bits and the week at which to resume inside that record in its high 128 bits. **Only the referrer's own flush writes the cursors, and they only advance.** A third party (an allowlisted bot) ships weeks above both stored cursors, must wait for the referrer's first own flush before it can touch a virgin book, and is refused during the one day outage grace after a vault pause is lifted or the recorder is re armed. `CohortReferrerFlushed` carries the cursor values after every own flush.

A virgin cursor anchors at the current week minus 103, so the first flush covers at most the last 104 closed weeks; `advanceBook` is the permissionless way to pre walk a dormant book before that. A batch of more than one referrer takes only books whose settle crosses no week boundary (`FlushBookNotAdvanced` otherwise), so one call never walks more than one book's boundaries. A single referrer settles inline.

The planner works within the envelope caps: 16 entries, 160 work units on a spoke, and on the hub chain `LOCAL_FLUSH_ENTRIES` (6) and `LOCAL_FLUSH_WORK` (96), because that transaction also pays the hub intake. `GAP_WORK_RESERVE` (40) units are held back for the gap lane so a long dormant span keeps making progress beside the weekly crumbs. The deliverer packed into the header is the caller when the caller is an allowlisted flush bot, otherwise zero in a CCIP envelope and the referrer itself on the hub chain, whose intake refuses a zero deliverer. The CCIP message carries `flushGasLimit` (default 300,000, at most 2,000,000) and the fee is paid by the caller with the excess refunded. `quoteFlushFee` and `previewFlushEnvelope` are no settle lower bounds for planning.

### The Hub: Buckets per Source and Referrer

Sources are keyed by `bytes32`. A local reporter is `bytes32(uint256(uint160(reporter)))`; a remote source is `keccak256(abi.encode(chainId, vault))` stamped by the mailbox from the message's authenticated origin. `sourceKeyOf(chainId, vault)` computes both. A source's chain id is written at its first delivery (`sourceChainIdOf`) and keys the claimable bucket the source credits.

For every source, referrer and closed week the hub keeps `sourceWeekOf` (the reported referred average in the low half, the commission units already paid in the high half), `sourceSelfOf` (the reported self average) and `paidCursor` (the three lane cumulative quantity already priced). `weekInputsOf[referrer][week]` sums the referred averages in its low half and the self averages in its high half over every source; those two sums are the ladder inputs of the week. **Every report for a closed week is final and merged by maximum, so a bucket only rises and a week's rates never fall.**

A delivery raises the week's bucket if its average is higher, then prices the increment of each lane above the cursor at the week's current rates: due is `floor((y1 * y1Bps + y2 * y2Bps + m * mBps) / (10,000 * YEAR))` over the cumulative cursor, the cell is paid `due - paidSoFar`, and the referrer's bucket for the source's chain rises by it. A report that raises a week's rates emits `WeekRatesRaised` and tops up its own source's cell inline; every other source already paid for that week is topped up by the permissionless, idempotent `settleWeeks(referrer, fromWeek, weekCount)` (at most 104 weeks per call), so the settled price of a week never depends on the order its sources arrive in. A lane below its cursor emits `CrumbLaneRegression` and pays nothing.

The courier fee is `botShareBps` of the delivered increment's own due, taken from the rate table in force for the week, clamped by `delivererShareCap` when that cap is nonzero, credited to the deliverer's bucket for the delivery's chain and counted in `totalAccrued` beside the commission. It is paid only when the deliverer is set and differs from the referrer, never on a top up.

A bucket counts toward `totalPayable`, the amount the pot owes, only once its owner has a claim path: a hub chain bucket from `setPayoutAddress` or its owner's first claim, a spoke bucket from its first landed designation (`PayoutBacklogReleased` when the accrued backlog is released). `totalPayable - totalClaimed` is the outstanding amount the pot should hold. `claim` pays a bucket in full and reverts `PotDry` when the hub holds less; `claimUpTo` and `claimAll` pay up to the hub's balance. A spoke bucket without a landed designation reverts `PayoutNotDesignated` and `claimAll` skips it.

The mailbox stores `flushCommitment[messageId]` as the hash of the source chain selector, chain id, vault and payload at receive time, and `applyFlush` re checks that commitment, that the source is still registered, the header versions, the entry count and, after the entries, the payload length and the work cap. A malformed envelope reverts as a whole and stays committed; a bad designation word is skipped with `FlushDesignationSkipped` and the entry still prices. A designation push from a registered registry is applied with the same latest wins rule as a piggybacked designation: a stamp `(setAt << 8) | rank` must exceed the stored one, and a stamp more than one hour ahead of the hub clock is skipped.

## Timelocks and Master Levers

**Every lane that lets a new party write is behind three days.** On the vaults the selector routing, the transfer hook pointer, the registry pointer and the flush hub pointer each have their own propose, execute and cancel trio with `SELECTOR_CHANGE_DELAY` (3 days), instant before the vault's setup is finalized. On the registry the sync peers, the router, the payout hub route and new allowed vaults wait three days once `finalizeSyncSetup` ran; the correction lane waits three days by construction. On the hub, selector changes wait three days after `finalizeSetup`, and every grant waits the same delay through the lever timelock: `proposeLeverChange(bytes call)` records the hash of the exact setter call, `LeverChangeProposed` carries the full call so the pending grant is public for the whole delay, and the setter runs only as that exact call at least three days later, once. Grants are allowing a reporter, setting the router, setting a flush or designation source to a chain id and elevating a referrer. Revocations never wait: a reporter set to false, a source set to chain id zero, an elevation ended.

The master levers of the hub are these.

* **`pushRateTable(table)`:** appends a table for a future week, strictly after the last table's week and within 520 weeks of now. A table needs a cap within 10,000 basis points, a year two rate within the cap, a mature rate within the year two rate, tiers that never fall from one rung to the next, strictly rising positive thresholds and a courier share within `MAX_BOT_SHARE_BPS` (2,000). Past weeks keep their table.
* **`setDelivererShareCap(cap)`:** bounds the courier fee of each priced cell in USD units; zero means no cap and is the genesis value.
* **`elevateReferrer(referrer, fromWeek)`:** opens an elevation window at a future week, open ended, as a grant behind the lever timelock. It refuses a referrer whose latest window is still open and a referrer whose history already holds `MAX_ELEVATION_WINDOWS` (8) windows (`ElevationHistoryFull`).
* **`endElevation(referrer, untilWeek)`:** closes the open window at a future week at or after its start, at once. A new grant after an end appends a window instead of replacing the ended one, so every window keeps its verdict for good; `elevatedFromWeek` and `elevatedUntilWeek` mirror the latest window, and `ReferrerElevated` and `ReferrerElevationEnded` carry each window's bounds.
* **`setAllowedReporter(reporter, allowed)`:** names a hub chain vault as a reporter (the grant waits, the revoke does not).
* **`setCcipRouter`, `setFlushSource(chainSelector, vault, chainId)` and `setDesignationSource(chainSelector, registry, chainId)`:** wire the CCIP side. A source is never set to the hub's own chain id, is never re keyed to another chain id without a removal first, and chain id zero removes it.
* **`setSourceSystem(source, systemId)`:** tags a source with its vault system (below `MAX_SYSTEMS`, 8) for indexers and the umbrella's accounting; pricing does not read it and the tag freezes once the source has delivered.

> We can never write a score, a cursor, a week bucket or a claimable on the hub, can never change a referrer alone on the registry, and can never move a user's recorded stake on a vault except through the consent gated correction lane and the retroactive seed, which credits only a user's untracked balance from the seed instant on and dies with the registry's seed latch.

The vault and registry master functions, and what the upgrade changes among them, are listed in [Admin Functions and What the Referral Upgrade Changes](for-integrators.md#admin-functions-and-what-the-referral-upgrade-changes).

## Storage Rules

The vaults are selector routed diamonds with one inherited declaration chain shared byte for byte by the entry contract and every facet. **New state is only ever appended as a tail shard**; nothing is removed, reordered or retyped, and there are no gap reserves. The referral and cohort shards occupy slots 67 to 95 of the vault layout: the dormant in diamond `referrerOf` (67), `cohortSyncEnabled` and `trackedContribution` (68, 69), the book, settle stamp, two rings, week log and gap log (70 to 75), a retired payout accumulator (76), the registry pointer trio (77 to 79), a retired hub pointer trio (80 to 82), the flush shard (83 to 89), `attributedContribution` (90), the divest park (91), the self crumb (92), the disarm span and outage stamps (93, 94) and the self log (95). Every shard follows the default off convention: a live diamond with zeroed tail storage behaves exactly as before until we arm the feature. The full table, the dated prose and the regeneration command are in `src/diamond/STORAGE_LAYOUT.md`; `script/check_storage_layout.sh` proves in CI that the diamond and every facet compile to the same layout and that no slot or byte has two owners.

The hub is outside that shared chain: it is never delegatecalled by a vault and shares no storage with one. Its own layout, 40 entries from `master` at slot 0 to `elevationWindowsOf` at slot 39, is checked by the same script against `test/hub/storage_snapshot/hub_layout.json`, and it grows only at its tail; state that later designs retired stays declared and unused. The registry owns its layout outright (slots 0 to 29, listed in `src/registry/pending/README.md`) and is never repointed by a vault once the recorder is armed.

## The Read Surface and the Events

The reader, `ReferralHubReader`, batches the hub's reads for dashboards and bots; the hub, the registry and the vaults expose their primitives. Every view with its signature, the chain it is called on and the statistic it feeds is in [Reference: Every Read Function](ui-integration.md#reference-every-read-function), and every event with its indexed fields and the statistic a dashboard derives is in [Reference: Every Event](ui-integration.md#reference-every-event).

> Call every view through the diamond, never on a facet address directly, because a facet called directly reads its own empty storage.

The events an indexer needs most are `ReferralAssigned` on the registry for every first assignment, `CohortContributionCredited` on a vault for deposit attribution, `CohortFlushSent` and `CohortReferrerFlushed` on a vault and `FlushReceived` and `FlushApplied` on the hub for the flush pipeline, `CrumbPriced`, `GapPriced` and `WeekToppedUp` on the hub for the weeks priced, `DelivererSharePaid` for the courier share, `PayoutAddressSet` and `PayoutClaimed` for designations and claims, the correction events on the registry and `ReferrerElevated` and `ReferrerElevationEnded` on the hub, which replayed per referrer reconstruct the whole elevation window history.

## Vault Systems

One hub prices every vault system of the umbrella under one rate table, one cap, one ladder, one courier share and one payout pot. The World Mobile vaults are system 0 and the Sovren vault (`wsnusdc` on mainnet, built on the same vault code) is system 1; a later node system is added by a configuration record, a registry allow lane and a hub source tag (`setSourceSystem`, read by `sourceSystemOf`, informational only), never by a redeploy. A referrer's ladder inputs sum over every vault of every system. **Money never moves between systems**: no vault of one system is ever peered with a vault of another for moves or bridges, on any chain, which the deploy preflight reads in both directions on every run. The Sovren vault joins after the World Mobile rollout through its own registry and flush rounds, the mainnet registry's allow lane and a hub reporter grant behind the lever timelock; until then its configuration record is marked deferred, which keeps its identity in every mainnet preflight and skips its rollout items.

### Conclusion

The vaults measure, the registry agrees and the hub prices and pays. Every write path that could move money or attribution is either permissionless and idempotent, or behind a three day timelock that is public from the moment it is queued, and every bound a loop depends on is a constant you can read next to the loop. Build on the diamonds and the reader, and take every signature from the UI integration chapter.
